The Long-Short Advantage

Unlike traditional Mutual Funds that only benefit when stocks go up, SIFs utilize a Long-Short framework. This allows managers to "hedge" the portfolio.

"In a volatile Indian market, the ability to short up to 25% of the portfolio acts as a 'shock absorber,' often preserving capital when the broader index faces double-digit drawdowns."

SEBI limits naked short positions to 25% to protect the portfolio from extreme volatility. Funds cannot take positions exceeding their capital, preventing destructive speculation and margin calls.

Focus on Risk-Adjusted Returns

SIFs aim for downside protection and steady income rather than outsized returns. Consistency over speculation.

  • Long-short flexibility — generating absolute returns in all market cycles
  • SEBI-regulated with MF-like taxation benefits
  • No leverage — prevents destructive speculation

Understanding SIF Investment Returns

When analyzing SIF investment returns, it's crucial to look beyond absolute numbers and focus on the risk taken to achieve them. The returns are generated through a mix of long equity positions (benefiting from growth) and short positions (hedging risk).

Historically, SIF investment returns aim to exhibit lower volatility compared to standard equity mutual funds, providing a smoother ride during market corrections while still targeting inflation-beating growth.

Key Drivers of SIF Performance

To evaluate SIF performance accurately, investors should understand the primary factors that drive their returns and risk profiles:

  • Hedging & Downside Protection: The 25% naked short cap allows fund managers to offset market drops, making SIF performance more resilient than traditional long-only mutual funds during bear markets.
  • Alpha Generation: Manager skill is reflected in active outperformance (Alpha) over the benchmark (Nifty 500 TRI), achieved through strategy rotation and individual stock selection.
  • Risk-Adjusted Ratios: Evaluating standard Sharpe and Sortino ratios helps distinguish funds that generate performance through high leverage or extreme risk from those achieving stable, asymmetric returns.

Top Performers Composite Scoring Model

To identify the absolute top-performing funds without ignoring the risks they take, Sanvasify calculates a unified Multi-Factor Composite Score (scaled from 0 to 100) using min-max normalization across all active schemes in our database. Here is how the score is calculated:

1. The Formula & Weightings

  • Alpha (α) — 25%: The excess return generated over the risk-adjusted benchmark. Higher alpha shows stronger manager skill.
  • Sortino Ratio — 20%: Measures returns relative to downside volatility (only penalizing drops, not positive swings).
  • Sharpe Ratio — 15%: Measures returns relative to total volatility (standard deviation).
  • Annualised Return — 10%: The absolute compounded growth.
  • Maximum Drawdown — 10%: The largest peak-to-trough drop in historical NAV. Smaller drops receive higher scores.
  • Beta (β) — 10%: Sensitivity to market movements. Lower beta (higher insulation) gets a higher score.
  • Capture Spread — 10%: The difference between Upside Capture and Downside Capture ratios.

2. Category Champions Definitions

Using these factors, we rank and group the funds into three specialized leaderboards:

  • 👑 The "Alpha King": Funds that generate the highest active risk-adjusted returns (highest Alpha values). Ideal for aggressive growth.
  • 🛡️ The "Shield Guardian": Insulated funds (market Beta < 0.5) that deliver the highest Sortino ratios. Ideal for capital preservation.
  • ⚡ The "Asymmetric Runner": Tactical hedge leaders with the widest spread between Upside Capture and Downside Capture ratios (gaining on positive market days, protecting on down days).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is a SIF better than standard Mutual Funds (MF)?

SIFs can engage in long-short hedging strategies, giving them a significant performance cushion during market downturns. Traditional mutual funds are "long-only" and suffer fully when the index declines. However, SIFs have a higher minimum investment threshold (₹10 Lakh vs ₹5,000) and are geared towards sophisticated investors seeking downside protection.

What are the minimum investment requirements for a SIF?

To invest in a Specialised Investment Fund (SIF) in India, the minimum investment requirement is ₹10 Lakh. This threshold is aggregated at the PAN level per Asset Management Company (AMC).

How does SIF performance compare to PMS and AIF Category III?

While Portfolio Management Services (PMS) require a ₹50 Lakh minimum and AIF Category III require ₹1 Crore, SIFs offer similar sophisticated long-short strategy benefits at a lower entry point of ₹10 Lakh. In addition, SIFs enjoy favorable mutual fund-like taxation (pass-through status) under Section 10(23D), whereas Category III AIFs are taxed at the fund level.

How long should I stay invested in a SIF?

SIF investment strategies are designed for medium to long-term horizons. A minimum investment period of 2 to 5 years is recommended to allow the fund manager's long-short hedging strategies to cycle through different market conditions and protect/grow capital.

Is a demat account mandatory for SIF investments?

No, holding a demat account is not mandatory. You can hold your SIF units directly in Statement of Account (SoA) format, exactly like standard mutual funds.

Who is eligible to invest in SIFs in India?

Resident Indian individuals, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), partnership firms, trusts, and private/public limited companies meeting the KYC requirements are eligible to invest in SIFs.

What is the subscription and redemption frequency for SIFs?

Although SIFs are open-ended, the subscription and redemption frequencies depend on the specific scheme. To protect fund liquidity, some strategies might allow daily subscriptions but restrict redemptions to a weekly or fortnightly schedule.

What are the key benefits of SIFs highlighted by NISM?

The National Institute of Securities Markets (NISM) highlights three primary SIF benefits: accessibility (offering institutional-grade hedging strategies at a lower entry threshold of ₹10 Lakh compared to PMS and AIFs), transparency (regulated pooled structures with daily NAV declarations), and tax efficiency (pass-through status per mutual fund laws, avoiding high fund-level taxes).

Are there any leverage or borrowing restrictions on SIFs?

Yes, SEBI strictly prohibits SIFs from taking leverage. SIFs cannot borrow funds to build speculative exposure, protecting investors from amplified losses. All derivative positions must be backed by cash or cash equivalents, and naked short positions are capped at 25% of the portfolio.

Who are SIFs best suited for according to regulatory guidelines?

SIFs are suited for sophisticated investors, high-net-worth individuals (HNIs), and retail investors who understand derivative strategies, can commit a minimum aggregated investment of ₹10 Lakh, and seek risk-managed capital protection and alpha-generation rather than simple passive index tracking.
